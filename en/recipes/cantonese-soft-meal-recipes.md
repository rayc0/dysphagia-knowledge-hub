# Cantonese meal ideas for a prescribed texture-modified diet

**Use this page as a planning checklist, not a tested recipe.** A dietitian and speech-language pathologist (SLP) should adapt each idea to the person’s care plan and assess the prepared dish. Cantonese meal names provide context, but they do not establish texture or suitability. Nothing here assigns an IDDSI level.

## Understanding texture checks

The [IDDSI Testing Methods (July 2019)](https://www.iddsi.org/images/Publications-Resources/DetailedDefnTestMethods/English/V2TestingMethodsEnglish31july2019.pdf) describe tests for a product at testing time and say to use intended serving conditions, especially temperature. The full applicable test—not a recipe name, blender, processing time, or appearance—is needed to assess the prepared item. IDDSI states that a clinician should select foods and drinks based on a comprehensive assessment. Record the prescribed texture and care-team instructions before developing a dish.

## Editorial planning checklist

### 1. Choose an ingredient or flavor idea

- [ ] **Congee:** Discuss a congee-based meal and suitable ingredients with the care team; a congee name does not establish its texture.
- [ ] **Steamed egg:** Discuss an egg-based dish with the care team. List every addition because it may need separate assessment.
- [ ] **Fish:** Consider cooked fish with ginger and permitted sauce or garnish ideas. Ask whether the accompaniment should be mixed, served alongside, or omitted.
- [ ] **Tofu:** Consider the tofu texture specified in the care plan, with cooked greens or another permitted accompaniment. Record whether each component may be combined.

These are shopping concepts only. They are not instructions, and each recipe must be developed and tested individually.

### 2. Shop from the approved list

- [ ] Confirm ingredients, seasonings, allergens, and any prescribed restrictions with the dietitian.
- [ ] Ask the SLP which components are appropriate for the required texture.
- [ ] Note each ingredient’s starting texture and whether it needs separate treatment; product names alone do not answer this.
- [ ] Keep the shopping list ingredient-focused rather than adding unconfirmed measurements or products.

### 3. Prepare a testable version

- [ ] Develop the recipe with the dietitian and SLP; this page does not provide a method or guarantee safe preparation.
- [ ] Do not include raw or undercooked food.
- [ ] Keep mixed components separate until the care plan says how they may be served together.
- [ ] Treat blending as one possible preparation method, not proof of texture; see the [blender guide](/en/equipment/blenders-for-texture-modification/).
- [ ] Follow the care plan for storage and reheating, and use the [food storage guide](/en/caregiving/storing-reheating-texture-modified-meals/) for related handling information.

### 4. Ask before testing

- [ ] Which full IDDSI test or prescribed texture description applies?
- [ ] Which ingredients and flavor ideas are allowed?
- [ ] Must each component be checked separately as well as the mixed dish?
- [ ] At what serving temperature will the meal be assessed and served?
- [ ] Do holding, mixing, or reheating change what the care team expects?

### 5. Check the actual meal

- [ ] Prepare the individualized version and apply the full applicable IDDSI test under the intended serving conditions.
- [ ] Check the food in the form and temperature in which it will be served.
- [ ] Follow care-plan guidance for separate components, sauces, and garnishes in a mixed dish.
- [ ] Record the recipe, serving conditions, and result. Do not infer texture from appearance or a dish’s name.
- [ ] If the result is uncertain, ask the dietitian or SLP before treating the meal as matching the prescribed plan.

This keeps Cantonese meal ideas useful while leaving texture decisions to individualized assessment and testing.
