# Storing and Reheating Texture-Modified Meals: A Caregiver Checklist

Texture-modified food still needs careful time-and-temperature control. A texture check addresses how food is prepared for swallowing, but it does not establish whether the food is safe. This checklist helps home caregivers store, reheat, and verify meals while following the person’s clinician-prescribed texture plan.

## Before Storing or Serving

- **Confirm the prescribed texture.** Check the person’s care plan for the required food texture and drink consistency before preparing, storing, or serving a meal.
- **Check the final serving temperature.** Reassess the texture at serving temperature using the appropriate IDDSI test method for the prescribed texture level. IDDSI specifies testing under the intended serving conditions, especially temperature ([Testing Methods, page 2](https://www.iddsi.org/images/Publications-Resources/DetailedDefnTestMethods/English/V2TestingMethodsEnglish31july2019.pdf)).
- **Ask when uncertain.** If the correct texture level, test method, or effect of reheating is unclear, contact the person’s clinician, dietitian, speech-language pathologist, or care team before serving.

The storage and reheating limits below follow U.S. FoodSafety.gov home guidance. Follow any product-specific instructions and the person’s care plan.

## Storage Checklist

1. **Keep the refrigerator at 4°C or below and the freezer at -18°C or below** (https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety).
2. **Refrigerate promptly.** Place food in shallow containers and refrigerate it promptly. Divide larger portions into shallow containers rather than storing them in one deep container (https://www.foodsafety.gov/people-at-risk).
3. **Use the room-temperature limits.** Discard perishable leftovers left out for **more than 2 hours at room temperature**, or **more than 1 hour when the temperature is above 32°C** (https://www.foodsafety.gov/people-at-risk).
4. **Use cooked leftovers within 4 days.** Record the date the food was prepared and refrigerated so the storage period is easy to follow (https://www.foodsafety.gov/people-at-risk).
5. **Thaw safely.** Never thaw meat, poultry, or seafood on the counter. Follow the instructions for the particular product and ask the care team for help if a thawing method is unclear (https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety).
6. **Label each container.** Include the preparation date and the person’s prescribed texture so an incorrect meal is less likely to be served.

## Reheating Checklist

- **Reheat cooked leftovers to 74°C (165°F).** Use a food thermometer to confirm this temperature rather than judging only by appearance or the warmth felt through the container (https://www.foodsafety.gov/people-at-risk).
- **Distribute the heat evenly.** Stir or rotate the food during reheating, where appropriate for the prescribed texture, and use the thermometer to confirm that reheating has reached 74°C (165°F).
- **Allow the reheated food to cool to an appropriate serving temperature.** Then perform the appropriate IDDSI test at serving temperature. A meal that matched the required texture before heating may need adjustment afterward.

## Serving and Leftovers

- Keep the person’s prescribed texture, consistency, utensils, and positioning instructions at the center of each meal.
- Do not rely on smell, appearance, or a successful texture test to decide whether food is safe.
- If the food does not meet the required texture after storage or reheating, do not serve it in that form without guidance from the care team.

## Keep Safety and Texture Separate

An IDDSI test evaluates characteristics such as the food’s flow or how it breaks apart. It does **not** test for harmful bacteria, time-temperature exposure, or overall food safety. Food-safety checks and texture checks answer different questions, and both are needed.

## Questions for the Care Team

Ask the care team which IDDSI level and method should be used for each food and drink, how reheating should be adjusted for the prescribed texture, and what plan applies when a prepared meal must be frozen or portioned. Keep the care plan with the household’s meal-preparation records and update it whenever the clinician changes the prescribed texture.
