# Corticobasal Degeneration (CBD) and Dysphagia — Clinical Guide to Swallowing Problems in Corticobasal Syndrome

> **TL;DR:** Corticobasal degeneration (CBD) is a rare 4-repeat tauopathy and one of the three main "atypical parkinsonism" syndromes, alongside progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP) and multiple system atrophy (MSA). Swallowing problems appear later in CBD than in PSP — a median of about 64 months (roughly 5 years) after symptom onset in autopsy-confirmed series — but they are common by the advanced stages, with studies reporting dysphagia in 31–96% of patients depending on how it is measured. The most characteristic instrumental findings are a fragmented, piecemeal oral swallow and residue pooling in the valleculae. Because dysphagia in CBD signals bulbar involvement and shortened survival, early instrumental assessment (VFSS or FEES), texture modification using the IDDSI framework, and proactive advance care planning are the cornerstones of management.

## What is corticobasal degeneration?

Corticobasal degeneration is a rare, progressive neurodegenerative disease caused by abnormal accumulation of 4-repeat tau protein in the cortex and basal ganglia. It belongs to the family of "atypical parkinsonian disorders" (also called Parkinson-plus syndromes), together with PSP and MSA.

CBD is substantially rarer than PSP — estimated at roughly one-tenth the frequency — with an incidence of approximately 0.6–0.9 per 100,000 people per year and a prevalence of about 5–7 per 100,000. It accounts for roughly 4–6% of all parkinsonism. Typical age of onset is around 60–64 years, and mean survival from first symptoms is approximately 6.5–7 years [Armstrong 2013; PMC8288513].

A key source of confusion is the distinction between **corticobasal degeneration (CBD)** — the underlying tau pathology, confirmed only at autopsy — and **corticobasal syndrome (CBS)** — the clinical picture of asymmetric rigidity, dystonia, myoclonus, apraxia, cortical sensory loss and the "alien limb" phenomenon. Only about half of people with clinical CBS turn out to have CBD pathology; the rest have Alzheimer's disease, PSP, frontotemporal lobar degeneration or other pathologies. Conversely, CBD pathology can present as a behavioural or language syndrome without classic motor signs. The Armstrong 2013 criteria (Neurology 2013;80:496–503) formalised this by defining probable and possible CBD across four phenotypes: CBS, frontal behavioural-spatial syndrome, nonfluent/agrammatic primary progressive aphasia, and a PSP-like syndrome [Armstrong 2013].

This clinicopathological mismatch matters for dysphagia: most swallowing studies enrolled patients with the *clinical syndrome* (CBS), so the figures below blend true CBD with look-alike pathologies.

## How common is dysphagia in CBD?

Reported prevalence varies widely depending on the method used:

- **13%** — early clinical series relying on patient report (Riley et al., 1990)
- **31%** — autopsy-confirmed CBD series, median disease duration 64 months (Müller et al., 2001)
- **86%** — videofluoroscopy (VFSS) series in clinically diagnosed patients (Frattali & Sonies, 2000)
- **96%** — swallowing questionnaire (NIH Speech Pathology tool) in CBS patients with mean disease duration of 46 months (Grunho et al., 2015) [JMD 2019 review]

In the Grunho ultrasound/VFSS study, 61% of CBS patients showed prolonged oro-pharyngeal swallow duration on ultrasound, and 27% had an abnormal swallow on VFSS — rising toward 100% when mild-to-moderate abnormalities were included [JMD 2019].

The practical message: by the middle stages of the disease, most patients have measurable swallowing impairment even if they do not complain of it. Subjective under-reporting is common across the atypical parkinsonisms, so absence of complaints should not be taken as absence of dysphagia.

## When does dysphagia appear — and why does timing matter?

The most-cited timing data come from Müller and colleagues' 2001 postmortem series of 83 pathologically confirmed parkinsonian cases (JAMA Neurology / Arch Neurol 2001). In CBD (n=13):

- **Dysarthria** appeared at a median of **40 months** after onset (92% of cases)
- **Dysphagia** appeared at a median of **64 months** (31% of cases)
- The median gap between speech and swallowing problems was **22 months**
- Median overall survival was **98 months**; median survival after dysphagia onset was **49 months** — though based on only 4 cases

Comparison across disorders from the same series:

| Disorder | Dysphagia latency (median) | Dysphagia prevalence |
|---|---|---|
| Parkinson's disease | 130 months | 41% |
| **CBD** | **64 months** | **31%** |
| MSA | 67 months | 73% |
| PSP | 42 months | 83% |
| Dementia with Lewy bodies | 43 months | 21% |

Two clinically useful rules emerge. First, dysphagia within the first year of motor symptoms is 100% specific for an atypical parkinsonism rather than Parkinson's disease — no CBD patient in this series developed dysphagia in year one, but when early dysphagia does occur in a "parkinsonism" patient, PD is effectively excluded. Second, latency to dysphagia correlated strongly with total survival (Spearman ρ = 0.88): the later swallowing fails, the longer the disease course. Dysphagia in CBD is believed to reflect bulbar dysfunction, which is uncommon early in the disease [Müller 2001].

## What does the swallow look like on instrumental testing?

The characteristic VFSS signature of CBS/CBD differs subtly from PSP:

- **Oral phase:** *piecemeal swallow* — the bolus is transferred in fragments over multiple tongue movements rather than as one cohesive unit. This is the single most frequent finding.
- **Pharyngeal phase:** *residue pooling in the valleculae* (the pockets between the tongue base and epiglottis) is characteristic.
- By contrast, PSP more typically shows a *delayed swallow initiation*, and MSA more often shows *crico-pharyngeal (upper esophageal sphincter) relaxation problems* [JMD 2019].

The proposed mechanism is disruption of neural pathways running from the anterolateral cortex and subcortical structures down to the brainstem swallowing centres — consistent with the cortical apraxia that defines the syndrome. In practical terms, the oral-phase fragmentation resembles an "apraxia of swallowing": the bolus is not lost because of weakness alone, but because the coordinated motor programme for transferring it is degraded.

Because aspiration can be silent in neurodegenerative disease, instrumental assessment — VFSS or FEES — is recommended when CBS patients report coughing, wet voice, weight loss or recurrent chest infections, and arguably at diagnosis given the high rate of subclinical impairment.

## How does CBD dysphagia differ from PSP, MSA and PD?

| Feature | CBD | PSP | MSA | PD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dysphagia prevalence | 31–96% (method-dependent) | 78–89% | ~73% | 41% (autopsy series) |
| Typical latency | ~64 months | ~42 months | ~67 months | ~130 months |
| Dominant VFSS pattern | Piecemeal oral swallow, vallecular residue | Delayed swallow initiation, oral-phase predominance | Crico-pharyngeal dysfunction, vocal fold impairment | Oral residue, piecemeal deglutition |
| Post-dysphagia survival | ~49 months (small n) | 15–24 months | 15–24 months | 15–24 months |
| Early dysphagia (<1 yr) | Not seen | Red flag, 100% specific for atypical | Red flag | Effectively excludes PD |

Sources: Müller 2001; JMD 2019; Glinzer 2024 (PSP meta-analysis).

The clinical takeaway: CBD sits between PSP (early, severe, universal) and PD (late, milder) in its swallowing trajectory. A CBS patient with early, prominent dysphagia should prompt diagnostic re-evaluation — the phenotype may actually be PSP pathology presenting as CBS, which the Armstrong criteria explicitly recognise.

## Managing dysphagia in CBD

There is no disease-specific dysphagia treatment for CBD, and no pharmacotherapy has proven benefit for the swallow itself. Management is symptomatic and follows the standard neurodegenerative-dysphagia playbook [JMD 2019; Practical Neurology 2021]:

1. **Instrumental assessment early.** VFSS or FEES at first symptom report — or at diagnosis — establishes a baseline and detects silent impairment. Repeat when symptoms change.
2. **Texture modification via IDDSI.** As oral-phase fragmentation progresses, cohesive, moist textures that hold together as a single bolus (IDDSI Level 5 minced & moist, then Level 4 puréed) reduce the demand on fragmented bolus transfer. Thin fluids are usually tolerated longer than in PSP, but should be tested instrumentally before thickening is imposed, given the quality-of-life costs of thickened fluids.
3. **Compensatory strategies.** Smaller bolus sizes, alternating solids and liquids, deliberate double swallows to clear vallecular residue, and upright positioning with post-meal upright time.
4. **Rehabilitative exercises.** Effortful swallow, Mendelsohn maneuver and tongue-strengthening protocols may be trialled, though evidence in CBD specifically is absent — they are extrapolated from stroke and PD literature.
5. **Nutrition and weight monitoring.** Weight loss is an early warning sign; dietitian involvement and oral nutritional supplements (matched to the safe IDDSI consistency) should start before significant loss occurs.
6. **Oral care.** Meticulous oral hygiene reduces the bacterial load of aspirated material and is one of the few interventions with randomised-trial support for preventing aspiration pneumonia in dependent adults (Yoneyama 2002).

## The feeding-tube question

A UK national survey of clinicians on gastrostomy (PEG) use in atypical parkinsonian syndromes found wide practice variation and no consensus, reflecting the absence of survival-benefit evidence in these conditions [MDC3 2025 survey]. In CBD the picture is complicated by two factors: the relatively long post-dysphagia survival (~4 years in the Müller series, versus 15–24 months in PSP/MSA/PD) means a feeding tube may support nutrition for a meaningful period; but the frequent co-occurrence of cognitive and behavioural change (CBD overlaps the frontotemporal dementia spectrum) raises the same comfort-feeding dilemmas seen in advanced dementia.

Best practice is to raise the question **early** — while the patient can still participate in the decision — as part of advance care planning, rather than in a crisis after a first aspiration pneumonia. Documented wishes, a clear explanation that tube feeding does not prevent aspiration of saliva, and involvement of palliative care teams all improve the quality of this decision.

## Practical guidance for caregivers

- **Watch the speech.** Dysarthria precedes dysphagia by a median of ~2 years in CBD; new slurring is a cue to request swallowing assessment before eating problems appear.
- **Do not wait for complaints.** Most CBS patients with measurable impairment do not spontaneously report it. Ask specifically about coughing on liquids, food sticking, longer mealtimes and weight change.
- **Learn the IDDSI tests.** The fork drip test and spoon tilt test let families verify texture at home; the flow test checks drink thickness.
- **Keep meals calm and unhurried.** Piecemeal swallowing means each mouthful takes multiple movements; rushing increases residue and aspiration risk.
- **Plan ahead.** Discuss feeding preferences, hospitalisation wishes and palliative options early — CBD progresses, and decisions made calmly in advance are better than decisions made in an emergency department.

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. **Assuming "CBS = CBD".** Half of clinical CBS is not CBD pathology; prognosis and dysphagia trajectory differ. Ask the neurologist which pathology is considered most likely.
2. **Relying on patient report.** Subjective under-reporting is the norm; instrumentally verify before and after texture changes.
3. **Thickening fluids by default.** Unlike PSP, CBD patients often tolerate thin fluids longer; unnecessary thickening carries dehydration and quality-of-life costs (Robbins 2008 trial context).
4. **Treating dysphagia as a late-only issue.** Although later than in PSP, measurable impairment is present in the majority by mid-disease; baseline assessment belongs at diagnosis.
5. **Deferring the feeding-tube conversation until crisis.** Median survival after dysphagia onset is measured in years in CBD — there is time to plan, and that time should be used.
6. **Missing the diagnostic red flag.** Dysphagia within the first year of parkinsonism argues strongly against PD and should trigger specialist review.
